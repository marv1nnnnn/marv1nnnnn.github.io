1999 – 2006

tape 磁带

The first music I had came on tape, and a tape only goes one way. To hear a song again you wind it back, and you learn where it starts by ear: a gap of hiss, a click, a number on the counter you wrote down.

Every play wore it a little. The song I played most was the most worn: duller, a little unsteady, with a dropout in the second chorus that became part of the song.

Play it. Wind it back with the keys, or turn the pencil in the reel.

2006 – 2012

file mp3

Then music stopped being a thing and became a file. A player the size of a lighter held a few hundred of them, copied off someone else's computer. Half were called Track 01; the rest were named in an encoding the player could not read.

And I could skip. I skipped everything. To fit, a file threw away what it guessed I would not miss, and at 64 kbps you could hear it guessing: the cymbals turned to water.

2012 – 2019

a radio that learned me 私人兆赫

Then the radio came back, as an app. It played one song after another like a radio, but each one was chosen for me, out of everything, from what I had done before: a heart, a bin, a skip. For a few years I built things like it; I was a music recommendation engineer.

It was very good. Every heart made it better at me, and narrower. In the end it handed my own taste back to me, a little narrower each time, until there was nothing in it I had not already heard.

♥ keeps a song, the bin keeps it away, ⏭ skips. It learns from all three, and from doing nothing.

2019 – now

on the air 电台

So I went back to the radio: not one that learns me, one that doesn't care. Someone in a room in London or Los Angeles or Bethlehem, playing what they love for reasons of their own, at an hour that isn't mine.

NTS has a motto I keep: Don't Assume. I don't choose songs any more. I turn the dial.

The dial is live, west to east. Whatever you find is on the air now, and gone when it ends.
